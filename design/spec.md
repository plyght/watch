# semicentric — "Breach" · design study + spec

*Design lead brief, v1. Read alongside `design/board.html` / `board.png`. All dimensions in mm.*

## 1. Thesis

This watch is about a machine that refuses to hide. It is a classic diver in the One in the Chamber language — aged gunmetal, silver, brass — with one physical event on the dial: the dial plate has been **breached from the inside**. At 7 o'clock, exactly over the beating balance wheel, the metal has torn open into five sharp petals that fold outward toward you, and the longest one runs up to 2 o'clock to *become* the 2 o'clock index. Metallurgists call this star-shaped tear **petalling**. Here it happens to draw the semicentric emblem, and nobody needs to print it. The off-centre idea is built into the dial too: the breach sits off-centre, and the dial's fine engine-turned rings are centred on the breach, not on the hands. The whole face is organised around the mechanism, not the logo. "The machine in front of you should answer to you": you can see it working, and its heart is where the story starts.

## 2. Directions

**A. Impact (the in-progress adaptation): critique.** The material story is right: the Chamber case, the silver chapter ring and the brass accent. The idea is weak in three places. (1) A bullet-impact spider-web crack reads as *damage done to the watch*, which is the wrong message for a security company. (2) The crack field plus the engraving makes the dial busy, so the loud feature has to compete with its own background. (3) The emblem applied in polished gold over the aperture is the logo stuck on top, which the client has already rejected. The twisted hex bar bezel is also a close echo of Derschutze's rope. Keep its materials. Drop its narrative.

**B. Tumbler.** A diver styled as a safe door. The bezel is engraved like a combination dial. Under a fixed "fence" window, a brass disc driven by the seconds wheel carries a single gate notch, so once a minute the gate lines up: *unlocked*. Loud feature: the moving lock wheel. It is charming and it moves, but it is literal security merchandise with no semicentric DNA, and it could pass for a padlock brand's watch.

**C. Breach (recommended).** Described in the thesis: a diver case, the Chamber bracelet, and a cast dial plate petalled open around an open-heart balance, with small seconds at 4:30 as the "marine" sub-dial. Loud feature: the sculpted, three-dimensional burst with the live balance inside it.

**Why C.** It keeps everything the client liked: the diver silhouette, the Shatter idea of seeing the mechanism through the emblem, the Chamber band and its material mix, renders over sketches, and a sub-dial. It also fixes the "not unique" complaint. The flat cut-out from the current render becomes a sculpted physical object, the way the frog and the cartridge are. The emblem appears only as geometry, never as a print, and the story is "nothing hidden", not "shot".

## 3. Spec: Breach

### Case
- Ø **41.0** (mid-case), bezel Ø 40.5. Overall thickness **11.6** (case 9.6 + crystal dome 2.0). Lug-to-lug **48.0**. Lug width **20**.
- Profile: Chamber-like "has some sides". The flank is a straight vertical band 4.2 tall, horizontally brushed, then aged. Lugs are straight-cut, slightly down-turned (6° from 10 mm out), each 6.0 wide at the tip. They have flat brushed tops with a 0.8 × 45° chamfer along the top outer edge, **polished and worn bright**. That chamfer is the only polished line on the case.
- Crown at 3: Ø 7.0 × 4.0, screw-down, 12 deep flutes (Chamber-style, chunky), plain end, no logo. **No crown guards**: they would clutter the clean Chamber flank, and the screw-down crown is protection enough.
- Finish: aged gunmetal. Blackened throughout, recesses stay black, and high points (lug edges, chamfers, bezel teeth tips, crown flutes) are rubbed back to bright steel.

### Bezel
- Unidirectional, 120-click. Grip edge: **60 sawtooth teeth**, each an asymmetric right-angle shard (vertical face clockwise, ramp anticlockwise), so the ratchet direction reads at a glance. It is functional and on-theme without being a logo.
- Top surface: solid metal, no insert. Slope 8°, width 4.5. Markings are engraved 0.15 deep and blackened: minute ticks; numerals 10–50 in Instrument Sans Medium, 2.2 tall; the first 15 minutes have 1-minute ticks.
- 12 o'clock pip: an inlaid **brass triangle** (2.4 wide) with a lume dot.

### Crystal
Double-domed sapphire, Ø 31.0 visible, 2.0 thick, inner AR coating. It sits 0.6 proud of the bezel.

### Dial (three layers, bottom to top)
1. **Dial plate** (Ø 31.6, base 0.5): cast brass, blackened gunmetal-black. The surface is **eccentric engine-turning**: concentric rings at 0.5 pitch, 0.06 deep, centred on the breach rather than the dial centre. It works like a vault-door finish and quietly says "off-centre".
2. **The breach**, cast integral with the plate. Centred on the balance at 7 o'clock, r = 7.5 from the dial centre (x −3.75, y −6.5). The central aperture is a five-point star polygon, roughly Ø 9, through which the balance is visible. **Five petals** follow the emblem geometry, rotated so the long blade points to 2 o'clock (~40° up from horizontal), scaled so its focal point lands on the balance pivot. They curl up to **0.45 max** at the tips, low enough to sit under the hand stack.
   - The long petal runs 19 long, from 2.0 wide at the root to a point, and its lume-filled tip **is the 2 o'clock index**. A 0.8-wide tear slot runs along its lower edge (the gap it tore out of), and the movement glints through it, the Shatter callback.
   - The petal top faces are blackened like the plate. The **torn edges and undersides are bright polished brass**, the fresh metal of the tear. That brass rim is the dial's single warm accent.
3. **Chapter ring** (OD 31.0, ID 27.4, 12° rehaut slope): **silver**, matte bead-blast. It carries an engraved, blackened minute track (60 ticks, the five-minute ticks longer). No numerals.

**Markers:** applied shard markers like the current render, which the client liked: angular, lume-filled frames from r 11.6 to 13.4. At 12 o'clock a double shard. **The 7 is missing**, because the breach ate it. **The 2 is the long petal.** All others are present.

**Small seconds at 4:30** (Ø 7.5, centre r 7.3): a recessed disc 0.2 deep, silver, finished with fine concentric snailing (centred on its own pivot). Ticks every 5 seconds. Its hand is a thin brass blade, the only moving brass.

**Hands:** the sword/blade style the client already likes, laser-cut, polished steel edges, lume-filled. Hour 9.5 long, minute 13.5 long (reaches the chapter ring), small seconds 3.3. Hole sizes for Miyota 82S5: 1.52 / 1.00 / 0.17.

### Caseback
Screw-down 316L. A Ø 18 sapphire window over the balance and escapement lets the beat be seen from both sides. The surrounding ring is brushed and aged, and engraved 0.1 deep in Inter, lowercase: `semicentric · answers to you · 01/01`.

### Bracelet: adapted One in the Chamber
- Same construction: three rows, plain outer links, raised centre plaques. 20 at the case tapering to 18 at the clasp. Link pitch 8.0, thickness 3.8. Centre plaque 8.5 wide, raised 0.4. Solid end links fitted to the case.
- **Change only the engraving.** Scroll filigree becomes **shard filigree**: an arabesque built from straight, tapering blades instead of curls. Each plaque's motif radiates from an *off-centre seed point* near one lower corner, and plaques alternate mirrored, so the band looks ornamental at arm's length and turns out to be geometric up close. Relief 0.3, recesses blackened, high points worn bright, exactly as on the Chamber.
- Outer links are brushed and aged, with plain polished chamfers.
- Clasp: fold-over with double push-buttons. The wordmark is engraved *inside* the blade.

## 4. Logo discipline

**May appear:**
- The emblem *as geometry*: the breach petals (dial) and the seed-point logic of the bracelet filigree.
- The wordmark, engraved and small, in two places only: the caseback ring and inside the clasp.

**May not appear:**
- No printed or applied emblem anywhere.
- No wordmark on the dial, crown, bezel, flanks, lugs or outer bracelet.
- No emblem on the crown.
- No red accents and no secondary "brand" colour.

Why: the story already is the logo. Printing it next to the breach would explain the joke.

## 5. Manufacturing and movement

**Movement: Miyota 82S5** (~$60): automatic, Ø 26.0 × 5.67, open balance at 7, small seconds at 4:30, 21,600 bph. It is the only buyable calibre whose fixed layout matches this design, and an 82S0 can serve as backup for prototyping. A spacer ring in the 41 case is needed: printed 316L, or Delrin.

| part | process |
|---|---|
| mid-case + lugs | LPBF 316L. Machine the movement seat, crystal/bezel seats, caseback thread, crown tube bore and spring-bar holes. Hand-finish, then age: black oxide, then hand-rubbed high points. |
| bezel | LPBF 316L. Turn the seat and click spring groove, cut the engraving, press in the brass pip. Ages with the case. |
| caseback | LPBF or turned 316L. Thread, window seat, laser engraving. |
| crown | turned 316L, fluted. Bought tube and gaskets (screw-down kit). |
| dial plate + breach | DLP-printed castable wax, lost-wax cast in brass (fine enough for 0.06 rings). Face-machine the back flat, cut the dial feet. Blacken, then hand-polish the petal edges and undersides. |
| chapter ring | turned brass, silver-plated (or sterling). Laser-engraved and blackened. |
| sub-dial disc | photo-etched nickel silver, snailed, silver-plated, recessed into the plate. |
| markers | photo-etched nickel-silver frames, lume-filled, pinned. |
| hands | laser-cut/etched steel, polished, lume. Small seconds hand etched brass. |
| crystal | off-the-shelf 31 double-domed sapphire. |
| bracelet | LPBF 316L links and plaques with relief printed in. Machine pin holes. Age to match. Bought fold clasp, engraved. |

Rough budget: movement $60, crystal and gaskets $70, case/bezel/back printing and machining $350, dial casting and finishing $150, hands/markers/etching $120, bracelet $300, ageing and assembly $200. **Total ≈ $1,250.**

## 6. Render plan

1. **Hero ¾** from 10 o'clock, low grazing key light from the upper left so the petals cast short shadows and their brass edges flare. Dark grey seamless background. This is the money shot.
2. **Dial macro**, straight on, 1:1. Eccentric rings, balance mid-swing (motion-blur the rim), lume tip on the long petal.
3. **Profile at 3 o'clock**: case flank, fluted crown, sawtooth bezel, crystal dome. This proves "some sides, not thick".
4. **Wrist shot** on a ~170 wrist, dark knit sleeve, overcast window light, bracelet in frame to show the shard-filigree plaques.

(Bonus: a lume shot, with only the shard markers and the petal tip glowing.)
